# 1.1 Arithmetic

## 1.1.1 Inequality & Comparison Symbols
### Concept Overview
Comparison symbols express the relationship between two quantities:

- **`=` (Equal to):** Both sides have the exact same value.
- **`≠` (Not equal to):** Both sides are not equal.
- **`<` (Less than):** The left side is smaller than the right side.
- **`>` (Greater than):** The left side is larger than the right side.
- **`≤` (Less than or equal to):** The left side is smaller than or equal to the right side.
- **`≥` (Greater than or equal to):** The left side is larger than or equal to the right side.

```{tip}
The open mouth of the symbol (`<` or `>`) always "eats" the larger number!
```


### Worked Examples

* **Example A:** Place the correct symbol between $\{math}`0.08` and $\{math}`0.8`.
  * *Step:* Compare place values. $\{math}`0.08` has a $\{math}`0` in the tenths place, while $\{math}`0.8` has an $\{math}`8`.
  * *Result:* $\{math}`0.08 < 0.8`

* **Example B:** Place the correct symbol between $\{math}`-5` and $\{math}`-2`.
  * *Step:* On a number line, $\{math}`-5` lies further left than $\{math}`-2`, meaning it is smaller.
  * *Result:* $\{math}`-5 < -2`

## 2. Decimals & Operations

### Key Rules
- **Multiplication:** Multiply as whole numbers, then count the total decimal places from both numbers to place the decimal point in the final answer.
- **Division:** Multiply both the dividend and divisor by $10, 100,$ or $1000$ to turn the divisor into a whole number before dividing.

### Worked Example
$$1.2 \times 0.04 = 0.048$$

## Lesson 2: Decimals & Operations

### Concept Overview

#### 1. Addition & Subtraction
- **Rule:** Line up the decimal points vertically, pad any missing trailing places with zeros, and add or subtract as normal.

#### 2. Multiplication
- **Rule:** Ignore the decimal points and multiply the numbers as whole numbers. Count the total number of decimal places in the original numbers, then place the decimal point in the final product so it has that same total count.

#### 3. Division
- **Rule:** Make the divisor (the second number) a whole number by multiplying both numbers by $\{math}`10`, $\{math}`100`, or $\{math}`1000` (shifting the decimal point to the right). Perform long division as usual.

---

### Worked Examples

* **Example A (Multiplication):** Calculate $\{math}`1.4 \times 0.03`.
  1. Multiply ignoring decimals: $\{math}`14 \times 3 = 42`.
  2. Count decimal places: $\{math}`1.4` has 1 place, and $\{math}`0.03` has 2 places (total = 3 places).
  3. Move the decimal point 3 places left from $\{math}`42`: $\{math}`0.042`.

* **Example B (Division):** Calculate $\{math}`4.8 \div 0.12`.
  1. Convert divisor $\{math}`0.12` into a whole number by multiplying both numbers by $\{math}`100`:
     ```{math}
     4.8 \times 100 = 480
     ```
     ```{math}
     0.12 \times 100 = 12
     ```
  2. Divide: $\{math}`480 \div 12 = 40`.
