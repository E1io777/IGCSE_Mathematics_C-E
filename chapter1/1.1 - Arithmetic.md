# 1.1 Arithmetic

## Learning objectives

By the end of this section, you should be able to:

- Perform calculations involving decimals and fractions.
- Understand and use mathematical symbols of equality and inequality.
- Apply the correct order of operations.
- Convert fractions into decimals and decimals into fractions.
- Convert recurring decimals into fractions.
- Compare and arrange fractions and decimals in order of size.
- Solve problems involving money, measurements, averages and other real-life situations.

## 1.1.1 Mathematical symbols

Mathematics uses symbols to describe relationships between numbers.

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| \(=\) | Equal to | \(4+3=7\) |
| \(≠\) | Not equal to | \(4≠7\) |
| \(<\) | Less than | \(3<5\) |
| \(>\) | Greater than | \(8>6\) |
| \(≤\) | Less than or equal to | \(x≤5\) |
| \(≥\) | Greater than or equal to | \(x≥2\) |

```{tip}
The open mouth of the symbol (`<` or `>`) always "eats" the larger number!
```

For example, \(3`<`7\) means that 3 is less than 7, while \(8`>`6\) means that 8 is greater than 6.

## 1.1.2 Working with decimals

Decimals represent parts of a whole. Each digit after the decimal point has a place value.

For example, in \(24.583\):

- 2 represents 2 tens.
- 4 represents 4 units.
- 5 represents 5 tenths.
- 8 represents 8 hundredths.
- 3 represents 3 thousandths.

---
### Adding and Subtracting Decimals

When adding or subtracting decimals:

1. **Write the numbers vertically.**
2. **Align the decimal points, not the final digits.**
3. **Add zeros when needed so that each number has the same number of decimal places.**
4. **Calculate from right to left, carrying or exchanging where necessary.**
5. **Place the decimal point in the answer directly below the other decimal points.**

---
#### Worked example 1: Calculate \(7.604 + 18.79\).

Write \(18.79\) as \(18.790\), then align the decimal points.

```text
       1  1
       7.604
     +18.790
     ───────
      26.394
```

Working from right to left:

- Thousandths: \(4 + 0 = 4\).
- Hundredths: \(0 + 9 = 9\).
- Tenths: \(6 + 7 = 13\), so write 3 and carry 1.
- Units: \(7 + 8 + 1 = 16\), so write 6 and carry 1.
- Tens: \(1 + 1 = 2\).

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{7.604 + 18.79 = 26.394}
$$

---
#### Worked example 2: Calculate \(23.5 - 8.76\).

Write \(23.5\) as \(23.50\).

```text
       2  3 . 5  0
     - 0  8 . 7  6
     ──────────────
       1  4 . 7  4
```

Working from right to left:

- Hundredths: \(0 - 6\) is not possible, so exchange 1 tenth for 10 hundredths.
  
  Then \(10 - 6 = 4\).
- Tenths: after the exchange, \(4 - 7\) is not possible.

  Exchange 1 unit for 10 tenths, giving \(14 - 7 = 7\).
- Units: after exchanging, \(2 - 8\) is not possible.
  
  Exchange 1 ten for 10 units, giving \(12 - 8 = 4\).
- Tens: \(1 - 0 = 1\).

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{23.50 - 8.76 = 14.74}
$$

```{warning} Common mistake
Do not write the numbers with their final digits aligned. Always align the decimal points.
```

### Multiplying Decimals

Multiply decimals as if they were whole numbers first. 

Then count the total number of decimal places in the original numbers.

And place the decimal point in the answer.

---
#### Worked example: Evaluate $4.5 \times 0.12$.

First, ignore the decimal points and calculate $45 \times 12$.

```text
      45
    × 12
    ─────
      90    (45 × 2)
     450    (45 × 10)
    ─────
     540
```

The number $4.5$ has 1 decimal place and $0.12$ has 2 decimal places.

Altogether there are $1 + 2 = 3$ decimal places.

Place the decimal point three places from the right

$$
\boxed{4.5 \times 0.12 = 0.54}
$$

---
### Dividing the decimals

When dividing by a decimal, multiply both the dividend and divisor by the same power of 10 to make the divisor a whole number. Then perform the division.

---
#### Worked example 1: Evaluate $1.44 \div 0.3$.

Multiply both numbers by 10: 
$$(1.44 \times 10) \div (0.3 \times 10) = 14.4 \div 3$$

Now divide using long division

```text
        4.8
    ────────
3   |  14.4
      -12
      ───────
        2 4
      - 2 4
      ───────
          0
```

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{1.44 \div 0.3 = 4.8}
$$

---
#### Worked example 2: Evaluate $2.4 \div 0.003$.

Multiply both numbers by $1000$ so that the divisor becomes a whole number:

$$(2.4 \times 1000) \div (0.003 \times 1000) = 2400 \div 3$$

Now divide using long division:

```text
        800
    ─────────
3   |  2400
      -24
      ───────
        000
```

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{2.4 \div 0.003 = 800}
$$

```{attention} Remember
Whatever you multiply the divisor by, you must also multiply the dividend by.
```

Multiplication and division by 10, 100, and 1000 can be performed by moving the decimal point.

| Operation | Example | Result |

| Multiply by 10 | $3.46 \times 10$ | \(34.6\) |
| Multiply by 100 | $3.46 \times 100$ | \(346\) |
| Multiply by 1000 | $3.46 \times 1000$ | \(3460\) |
| Divide by 10 | $34.6 \div 10$ | \(3.46\) |
| Divide by 100 | $34.6 \div 100$ | \(0.346\) |
| Divide by 1000 | $34.6 \div 1000$ | \(0.0346\) |

### 1.1.3 Order of operations: **BIDMAS** or **BODMAS** 

When a calculation contains several operations, use BIDMAS or BODMAS to determine the order. 


***Note***: Indices and Orders mean the same thing.


| Letter | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Brackets | Calculate inside brackets first. |
| **I / O** | Indices / Orders | Calculate powers and roots. |
| **D / M** | Division and multiplication | Work from left to right. |
| **A / S** | Addition and subtraction | Work from left to right. |


Division does not always come before multiplication, and addition does not always come before subtraction.

Operations at the same level are completed from left to right.


---
#### Worked example: Evaluate $(8 + 4.5) \times 0.03$.

**Step 1: Calculate inside the brackets.**

$$
8 + 4.5 = 12.5
$$

**Step 2: Multiply.**

$$
12.5 \times 0.03 = 0.375
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{(8 + 4.5) \times 0.03 = 0.375}
$$

## 1.1.4 Fractions

A fraction represents part of a whole and is written as

$$
\frac{a}{b}, \quad b \neq 0
$$

Here

- \(a\) is the **numerator**, the number above the fraction line.
- \(b\) is the **denominator**, the number below the fraction line.

For example, $\frac{3}{5}$ means three parts out of five equal parts.

---
### Adding and subtracting fractions

Fractions can be added or subtracted directly only when they have the same denominator.

If the denominators are different, find a common denominator first. The lowest common multiple (LCM) of the denominators is often the most convenient choice.

---
#### Worked example 1: Evaluate 
$$
\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{5}
$$

**Step 1: Find a common denominator.**

The LCM of 4 and 5 is 20.

**Step 2: Rewrite both fractions.**

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 5}{4 \times 5} = \frac{15}{20}
$$

$$
\frac{2}{5} = \frac{2 \times 4}{5 \times 4} = \frac{8}{20}
$$

**Step 3: Add the numerators.**

$$
\frac{15}{20} + \frac{8}{20} = \frac{23}{20}
$$

Convert to a mixed number:

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{5} = 1\frac{3}{20}}
$$

---
#### Worked example 2: Evaluate

$$
2\frac{3}{8} - 1\frac{5}{12}
$$

**Step 1: Convert the mixed numbers into improper fractions.**

To convert a mixed number into an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and place the result over the original denominator
* For $2\frac{3}{8}$: Multiply whole number $2$ by denominator $8$, then add numerator $3$

$$
\frac{(2 \times 8) + 3}{8} = \frac{16 + 3}{8} = \frac{19}{8}
$$
  
* For $1\frac{5}{12}$: Multiply whole number $1$ by denominator $12$, then add numerator $5$

$$
\frac{(1 \times 12) + 5}{12} = \frac{12 + 5}{12} = \frac{17}{12}
$$

**Step 2: Find a common denominator and convert the fractions.**

The lowest common multiple (LCM) of 8 and 12 is 24. 

Convert each fraction so they share the denominator of 24

$$
\frac{19}{8} = \frac{19 \times 3}{8 \times 3} = \frac{57}{24}
$$

$$
\frac{17}{12} = \frac{17 \times 2}{12 \times 2} = \frac{34}{24}
$$

**Step 3: Subtract the numerators.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{57}{24} - \frac{34}{24} 
&= \frac{57 - 34}{24} \\
&= \frac{23}{24}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{2\frac{3}{8} - 1\frac{5}{12} = \frac{23}{24}}
$$

---
### 4.2 Multiplying fractions

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

$$
\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd}
$$

---
#### Worked example: Evaluate

$$
\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5}
$$

Multiply the numerators and denominators

$$
\frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 5} = \frac{6}{20}
$$

Simplify the result by dividing the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor (2):

$$
\frac{6 \div 2}{20 \div 2} = \frac{3}{10}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{10}}
$$

Simplify the result if possible.

---
### Dividing fractions

To divide one fraction by another, multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of a non-zero number is found by dividing 1 by that number. For example,

$$
\text{Reciprocal of } 6 = \frac{1}{6}
$$

For a fraction, turn it upside down:

$$
\text{Reciprocal of } \frac{a}{b} = \frac{b}{a}
$$

---
#### Worked example

Evaluate

$$
\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{9}
$$

**Step 1:** Keep the first fraction.

**Step 2:** Change division to multiplication.

**Step 3:** Turn the second fraction upside down (reciprocal).

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{9}
&= \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{9}{4} \\
&= \frac{18}{12} \\
&= \frac{3}{2} \\
&= \boxed{1\frac{1}{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

```{tip} Memory tip
Keep, change, flip.
```

## 4.4 Mixed numbers

A mixed number contains a whole number and a proper fraction, such as $2\frac{3}{5}$.

To convert a mixed number to an improper fraction:

$$
a\frac{b}{c} = \frac{ac+b}{c}
$$

For example,

$$
2\frac{3}{5} = \frac{2 \times 5 + 3}{5} = \frac{13}{5}
$$

When multiplying or dividing mixed numbers, converting them to improper fractions first usually makes the calculation easier.

---
## 4.5 Finding a fraction halfway between two fractions

To find the number halfway between two values, calculate their mean.

For values $a$ and $b$, the midpoint is

$$
\frac{a + b}{2}
$$

---
#### Worked example

Find the fraction halfway between $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{3}}{2} 
&= \frac{\frac{3}{3}}{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
$$

## 5. Fractions and decimals

A decimal is another way of expressing a fraction. The two forms can represent exactly the same value.

---
### 5.1 Converting a fraction into a decimal

Divide the numerator by the denominator.

$$
\frac{a}{b} = a \div b
$$

---
#### Worked example 1: Converting $\frac{7}{8}$ to a decimal

Evaluate $\frac{7}{8}$ by calculating $7 \div 8$ using long division (by writing $7$ as $7.000$):

```text
       0 . 8  7  5
    ─────────────
8   |  7 . 0  0  0
     - 6 . 4
     ───────
         0 . 6  0
       - 0 . 5  6
       ─────────
           0 . 0  4  0
         - 0 . 0  4  0
         ─────────────
               0  0  0
```
Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{7}{8} = 0.875}
$$

---
#### Worked example 2: Convert $\frac{1}{3}$ into a decimal.

Evaluate $\frac{1}{3}$ by calculating $1 \div 3$ using long division (by writing $1$ as $1.0000\ldots$):

```text
       0 . 3  3  3  3 ...
    ─────────────
3   |  1 . 0  0  0  0
     - 0 . 9
     ───────
         0 . 1  0
       - 0 . 9
       ─────────
           0 . 0  1  0
         - 0 . 0  0  9
         ─────────────
             0 . 0  0  1  0 ...
```

$$
1 \div 3 = 0.3333\ldots
$$

The digit 3 continues indefinitely. This is written as

$$
\boxed{0.\overline{3}}
$$

The bar above the 3 indicates that the digit repeats forever.

---
### 5.2 Converting a terminating decimal into a fraction

A terminating decimal has a finite number of decimal places.

* One decimal place means tenths.
* Two decimal places mean hundredths.
* Three decimal places mean thousandths.

---
#### Worked example 1

Convert $0.35$ into a fraction in its simplest form.

**Step 1: Write the decimal as a fraction using place value.**

Because $0.35$ has two decimal places (tenths and hundredths), the last digit sits in the hundredths column. 

This means we write the number over 100

$$
0.35 = \frac{35}{100}
$$

**Step 2: Find a common factor to simplify.**

Look at the numerator ($35$) and the denominator ($100$). 
* Any number ending in $0$ or $5$ can be divided evenly by $5$. 
* Since $35$ ends in $5$ and $100$ ends in $0$, both numbers share a common factor of $5$.

**Step 3: Divide the numerator and denominator by 5.**

$$
\frac{35 \div 5}{100 \div 5} = \frac{7}{20}
$$

**Step 4: Check if it can be simplified further.**

The number $7$ is a prime number, and $20$ is not divisible by $7$. Because they share no further common factors, the fraction is now in its simplest form.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\frac{7}{20}}
$$

---
#### Worked example 2

Convert $0.125$ into a fraction in its simplest form.

**Step 1: Write the decimal as a fraction using place value.**

Because $0.125$ has three decimal places (tenths, hundredths, and thousandths), the last digit sits in the thousandths column. This means we write the number over 1000:

$$
0.125 = \frac{125}{1000}
$$

**Step 2: Simplify by finding common factors.**

Both numbers end in $0$ or $5$, which means they can both be divided evenly by $5$. We can simplify this step-by-step:

* **First division by 5:**

$$
\frac{125 \div 5}{1000 \div 5} = \frac{25}{200}
$$

* **Second division by 5:**

$$
\frac{25 \div 5}{200 \div 5} = \frac{5}{40}
$$

* **Third division by 5:**

$$
\frac{5 \div 5}{40 \div 5} = \frac{1}{8}
$$

(Note: Alternatively, if you spot that 125 goes into 1000 exactly 8 times, you can divide the numerator and denominator straight by 125 in one step 
                                   $\frac{125 \div 125}{1000 \div 125} = \frac{1}{8}$ )

**Step 3: Check if it can be simplified further.**

Since the numerator is $1$, the fraction cannot be simplified any further.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{8}}
$$

#### Worked example 1: One recurring digit

Convert \(0.\overline7\) into a fraction.

**Step 1: Let the recurring decimal be \(x\).**

\[
x=0.7777\ldots
\]

**Step 2: Multiply by 10.**

\[
10x=7.7777\ldots
\]

**Step 3: Subtract the original equation from the new equation.**

\[
\begin{aligned}
10x-x&=7.7777\ldots-0.7777\ldots\\
9x&=7
\end{aligned}
\]

**Step 4: Solve for \(x\).**

\[
\boxed{x=\frac79}
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{0.\overline7=\frac79}
\]

#### Worked example 2: Two recurring digits

Convert \(0.\overline{23}\) into a fraction.

\[
x=0.232323\ldots
\]

Since two digits repeat, multiply by 100:

\[
100x=23.232323\ldots
\]

Subtract the original equation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
100x-x&=23.232323\ldots-0.232323\ldots\\
99x&=23
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{x=\frac{23}{99}}
\]

### 5.4 When only part of the decimal repeats

Sometimes the decimal contains a non-recurring part before the recurring digits begin.

For example,

\[
x=0.5343434\ldots
\]

Here, 5 does not repeat, but 34 repeats.

Multiply by 10 to move past the non-recurring digit:

\[
10x=5.343434\ldots
\]

Multiply by 1000 to align the recurring parts:

\[
1000x=534.343434\ldots
\]

Subtract:

\[
\begin{aligned}
1000x-10x&=534.343434\ldots-5.343434\ldots\\
990x&=529
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence,

\[
\boxed{x=\frac{529}{990}}
\]

The key idea is to multiply by suitable powers of 10 so that the repeating decimal portions line up and cancel when the equations are subtracted.

## 6. Comparing and ordering numbers

Fractions and decimals can be compared by converting them into the same form.

For example, arrange the following numbers in ascending order:

\[
\frac13,\quad 0.4,\quad \frac25
\]

Convert the fractions into decimals:

\[
\frac13=0.3333\ldots,\qquad \frac25=0.4
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{\frac13<0.4=\frac25}
\]

**Ascending order** means smallest to largest. **Descending order** means largest to smallest.
